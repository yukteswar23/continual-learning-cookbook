Every large model you have used was trained once, frozen, and shipped. After that it does not change. If the world changes, if you teach it something new in a conversation, if it fails at a task a hundred times, the weights stay exactly where they were.

The authors of Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning open their paper with the same observation: today’s AI systems “remain static after deployment”, and while they can adapt at inference time through retrieval or prompting, “they do not update their parameters to acquire new skills, internalize new knowledge, or improve from experience” . The TTT-Discover paper says it the other way round: “Most of today’s AI systems remain static after deployment, even though the world keeps changing. The high-level goal of continual learning is to enable AI systems to keep changing with the world, similar to how humans improve throughout their lives” .

Continual learning is the study of how to close that gap: how to let a learning system take in a stream of experience, one piece after another, and keep improving, without destroying what it already knew. Irie and Lake give it the plainest definition in their glossary: “learning new tasks, one after another, in a sequential manner” .

0.2How to read a recipe

This is a cookbook, so most chapters contain one or more recipe cards. A card has the same four parts every time:

  • You will need: the ingredients, meaning the setting, the data and the assumptions the method relies on.
  • Method: the algorithm, step by step, in the order you would actually run it.
  • The math: the paper’s own equations, typeset in LaTeX, with each symbol explained.
  • Chef’s notes: what the authors themselves say can go wrong.

Around the cards, the chapters explain where each idea comes from and what the experiments showed, using the papers’ original figures with their original captions and tables transcribed number for number. Each figure carries a credit line and a link to its source, and the References chapter lists every paper with its licence. Quotation marks mean the authors’ own words. Everything else is written for this book.
